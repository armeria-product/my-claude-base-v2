# Website image art direction

## Choose a decisive direction

Adapt the brief rather than forcing a recipe. A minimalist or Swiss brief needs
restraint, generous negative space, and almost no gradient. Editorial/fashion
work can use off-grid type, strong imagery, and asymmetry. Cinematic/luxury work
can use palette-matched full-bleed photography, vignettes, and a giant
statement. Product, fintech, and infrastructure work need clear product framing
and trust-driven hierarchy. Agency work may be deliberately poster-like.

Set a theme, background character, typography character, hero architecture,
section system, concept spine, and one second-read moment. Keep those choices
legible rather than mixing every option.

## Per-section variation

Use at least three composition anchors on a multi-section site. Good alternatives
to the habitual text-left/image-right hero include centered statement,
bottom-left over a readable image, bottom-right CTA cluster, top-left lead,
right-text/left-image, centered-low, stacked center, image-as-canvas, and
off-grid editorial offset.

Vary background modes across the page: solid plus inline asset, technical grid,
full-bleed image with overlay, editorial side image, duotone/graded photo,
tactile material, quiet radial vignette, or controlled color block. For a
non-minimal multi-section site, include at least one full-bleed, duotone, or
atmospheric moment and one calmer section. Gradients may add depth only when
they are low-chroma, palette-locked, and subordinate to hierarchy.

Use exactly four signature component families, selected for the concept:
masonry, cascading card deck, hover-accordion slices, clean bento, marquee,
polaroid arc, rhythm lines, off-grid editorial structure, product-panel stack,
quote wall, metrics strip, or layered crop frames. Use exactly two
motion-implied cues such as text reveal, pinned narrative, stagger, parallax,
accordion expansion, or fade-through. These are visual cues, not code tasks.

## Page packs and continuity

A practical 4-section pack is hero, features, proof, and CTA. An 8-section pack
is hero, trust, features, product showcase, use cases, testimonials, pricing,
and CTA. Extend it with problem/solution, workflow, integrations, FAQ, or footer
only when the brief calls for it.

Across every frame preserve the palette, type scale, CTA identity, radius logic,
image grade/material vocabulary, and tonal copy. Vary anchors, backgrounds,
size, density, and tempo without changing the brand. Each major section has a
funnel job: hook, proof, education, or action.

## Image-led restraint

Use photography, product visuals, editorial crops, or structured abstract forms
as part of hierarchy, not filler. Use at least two distinct crop types when a
multi-section concept needs imagery. Keep copy short and believable. Use charts
only where the product needs them; proof can be quotes, receipts, timelines, or
real workflow imagery.

Reject repeated centered blocks, identical card rows, blobs, default AI
gradients, excessive glass, generic fake brands, unreadable logo tickers, fake
KPI columns, long startup headings, gradient type, stock-like filler, and a
page that becomes a mood reel with no conversion path.

Before delivery, check count, horizontal format, one section per frame, hero
clarity, spacing, visual distinction, cross-section continuity, composition
variation, imagery, and the final CTA.
