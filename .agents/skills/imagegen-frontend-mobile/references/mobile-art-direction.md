# Mobile image art direction

## Platform and frame

Pick one dominant platform and keep its navigation and chrome coherent. iOS work
benefits from calm top areas, safe-area awareness, restrained sheets, clear tabs,
and elegant spacing. Android work benefits from clearer app-bar, bottom-nav,
list/card, sheet, and state rhythm. Cross-platform work keeps universal
navigation and safe-area logic without mixing the two languages.

Present screens in one consistent, subtle phone frame by default. Keep scale,
bezels, shadows, and outer margins even. The device supports the content; it
must not crop, overpower, or vary randomly between screens.

## Design bible and flow

Lock platform, frame, palette, typography, spacing, radius, icons, imagery,
texture, navigation, cards/lists, buttons, and shadows before rendering. A
multi-screen set must retain those rules while varying screen purpose,
composition, image-to-text balance, density, CTA placement, background, and
tempo.

Order screens as an actual user journey. Make the transition from one screen to
the next understandable through navigation or state. Generate a fresh readable
detail image instead of cropping a previous screen, board, or small card.

## Readability and mobile reality

The first screen has one focal point, short copy, and a clear action. Reserve
top status/title and bottom navigation/home-indicator regions. Do not place
critical content in unsafe zones. Use realistic tab bars, stack navigation,
sheets, segmented controls, app bars, and action priority; do not make every
control equally important.

Use readable title, body, label, and CTA scale. If type is small, reduce
content, increase spacing, split the task into another screen, or regenerate.
Prefer open grouping over nested cards, fake widgets, tiny labels, chart spam,
and decorative pills.

## Visual direction

Choose a focused theme, type character, structure bias, image treatment,
texture, palette logic, exactly four signature components, two decorative asset
families, and two motion-implied cues. The set may be richer than forced
minimalism but must remain clean and touch-friendly.

Use photography, illustration, product imagery, media frames, or atmospheric
backgrounds when they matter to the category. Give text over imagery a real fade,
mask, scrim, or clean safe area. Keep image frames repeatable and crops
controlled. Use one or two accents with an intentional palette; avoid muddy
color, default purple-blue fintech glow, random bright rainbows, raw glass
cards, generic icon-library energy, filler thumbnails, and template-like flows.

Category cues: fintech needs trust and number clarity; health needs calm metrics;
productivity needs task hierarchy; social needs profile/media rhythm; commerce
needs browse/detail/cart clarity; wellness benefits from softer material and
breathing room.

Before delivery, verify complete screen count, fresh individual renders,
platform fit, safe areas, logical flow, design-bible continuity, readable type,
believable navigation, clean frame margins, purposeful imagery, and a
non-generic but controlled visual system.
